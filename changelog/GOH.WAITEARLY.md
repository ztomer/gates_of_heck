* **`goh land --wait` exits by the land record on every path, names its exit on its last line, and repeats an unchanged state with how long (GOH.WAITEARLY).** Three lanes in one evening read a wait as finished while the land still ran. Their tool shell was zsh, where `${PIPESTATUS[0]}` is empty and `cmd | tail` exits with tail's 0, and the harness moved a 180 s call to the background and reported the whole command's exit. The wait itself also answered something other than the record on three paths. A SIGTERM or SIGHUP gave 143 or 129, and a SIGINT gave a KeyboardInterrupt. A background job inherits SIGINT ignored, so its wait never ended. A closed stdout made the last line raise BrokenPipeError and exit 1, which reads as red. A done record in a state it did not know also exited 1. Now 0 means landed, 1 means red, and 3 covers every other way out: its own timeout, SIGTERM, SIGINT (an inherited ignore included), SIGHUP, or an unknown state. A closed stdout silences the rest of the output but does not end the wait. The last line reads `goh land --wait ID: exit N, ...`, so the output alone carries the verdict. A state that has not changed is printed again every minute with how long it has lasted and how long the wait has run, so a batch member shows its gate and its time (`lib/landq_look.py`, `tests/test_landq_wait_exit.py`).
