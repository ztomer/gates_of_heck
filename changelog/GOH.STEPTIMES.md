* GOH.STEPTIMES: a gate's step times are rows. Every land and ship left one `*.timings.jsonl` per gate or step (608 files, 33 MB in two weeks, pruned with the logs, so no step had a trend): the file is now the run's spool alone, and its lines become goh-cache.db's `steps` when the run ends (`lib/steps_store.py`; `tools/land.sh`, the ship's last act, the pin sweep's), in one transaction, then the file goes. A killed run's, a refused write's and every file of before are taken in by the state sweep. One timestamp, the step's end in epoch seconds (four of five lines had none); the passing native checks under 250 ms of one parent are one row with their count, 130,738 lines in 33,510 rows and 10 MB. `goh land-profile`, `goh ship --status` and the step attribution read the table, and `steps_store.py report RUN [PART]` prints a run's.
* GOH.STEPTIMES: the timing ledger is goh.db's `spans` and `span_steps` (`lib/span_store.py`): a push, a land, a suite, a release and an end-to-end land, one timestamp (`at`, epoch) instead of an ISO text and a tenth of a second of the same instant, the repo by its key, a land's span by its run. A `ship` and a `land attempt` are read from `ships` and `lands`, where every one of their numbers already was (`lib/timing_derived.py`). `timing/ledger.jsonl` is the spool of a writer that could not reach goh.db; the next that does takes it along, the 3,470 rows of before with it. `goh budget` prints the same on both.
