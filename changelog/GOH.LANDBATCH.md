- The landing queue judges several lands of one repo with ONE gate, merge-queue style (GOH.LANDBATCH): with three lanes a lane waited about as long queued as gated (2026-11-17: 572 agent-minutes queued against 711 gated). The lander's head land request takes up to `GOH_LANDQ_BATCH` (default 3; `1` is the one-by-one queue) land requests of its repo behind it, holds the land lock, stacks them in queue order (each branch rebased onto the one below by `tools/land.sh`'s new stacking mode, `GOH_LAND_STACK_ONTO`/`_UPSTREAM`, with the row merge driver), and runs the tip's `land.sh` once: its pre-step, gate and fast-forward, under the lander's hold. Green lands every item, each done record naming its own commit (an ancestor of main) and `goh land --wait` returning per item. Red bisects: the halves are restacked and gated, a tree already gated red is not gated again, and a lone red item is red with its own log and its branch back at its enqueued commit; an item that conflicts with the stack below it is red (`conflict`) and the rest go on. Three items with the middle one red take 4 gate runs. Every record carries `batch` {id, items, gates}; `goh land --report` counts a batch's gates once (`gates`, `gates_per_landed`). `lib/landq_batch.py`, `tests/test_landq_batch.py`.
